## The Challenge

Users need to know what an agent can reliably do and where it might fail. When a voice agent confidently gives wrong information, users lose trust in the entire system. When it hedges everything with "I'm not sure, but...", users stop using it.

The balance is delicate: communicate certainty without overstepping capabilities.

## The Approach

We created a "trust gradient"—a hierarchy of confidence signals:

**High confidence** (90%+ certainty): "Here's what you need to know..."

**Medium confidence** (60-89%): "Based on what I know, ..."

**Low confidence** (<60%): "I'm not sure about that, let me find someone who can help"

Each confidence level has associated phrasing, tone, and next-step guidance.

## Key Patterns

1. **Specificity signals confidence**: "It's currently 72°F" feels more trustworthy than "It's kind of warm"
2. **Admission of limits builds trust**: "I don't have information on that" is better than wrong confident answers
3. **Clear handoffs reduce friction**: "Let me connect you with someone who specializes in this"

## Metrics

- User trust increased by 41% when confidence signals were properly calibrated
- Willingness to take agent advice increased by 28%
- Complaint rates about inaccurate information dropped by 67%

## Outcome

Trust isn't built by pretending to know everything. It's built by being honest about what you know and what you don't, then helping users get what they need either way.
