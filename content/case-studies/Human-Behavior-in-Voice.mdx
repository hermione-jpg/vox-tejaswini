You ask your friend a simple question.

**“Are we leaving at 7?”**

They pause.

“Umm… yeah. I think so.”
You notice the hesitation and plan to leave late as well.

Now imagine they answer quickly:

**“Yeah, definitely. We need to leave at 7.”**

You notice the confidence and hurry as well.

We constantly pick up on these small changes when we talk to people.

A pause tells us someone might still be thinking.

A rushed answer tells us they might be in a hurry.

A repeated question tells us they may not have understood.

An interruption can tell us we're taking too long.

A suddenly short response can tell us they're losing patience.

A voice agent that only understands the words has understood only part of the interaction.

## Can a voice interface recognize changes in human behavior and adapt its interaction accordingly?

Voice interaction has a problem visual interfaces largely avoid:

In a visual UI, uncertainty might produce a pause before clicking. 
In voice, the user's behavior is part of the interface.

- “umm…”
- “wait”
- repeated words
- slower speech
- rising/falling pitch
- unfinished sentences
- corrections
- silence
- talking over the agent
- asking the same thing again
- abandoning the conversation

These aren't merely _speech characteristics_. They are **interaction signals**.

Designing adaptive voice experiences requires looking beyond speech recognition.

The system needs to observe these signals in three dimensions simultaneously:

**What was said.  
How it was said.  
What has been happening in the conversation.**

These become the three lenses for understanding behavioral state:
### Acoustic

The physical characteristics of speech: How the speech sounds.

**pace · pauses · pitch · energy · silence · overlap · response timing**

### Linguistic

The structure and content of language:

**fillers · repetitions · corrections · uncertainty markers · imperatives · questions · negations**

### Conversational

What is happening between turns:

**failed attempts · interruptions · repetition · repair · turn length · task progress · unanswered questions · abandonment**

None of these signals tells us exactly what a person is feeling.

Instead, together they provide **evidence about how the interaction is going**.
