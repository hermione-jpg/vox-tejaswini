In text chat, a two-second delay is barely noticeable. In voice, a two-second delay feels like the system froze.

## Where latency comes from

A single voice exchange passes through several stages, and each one adds time:

- Capturing and detecting speech
- Converting speech to text (STT)
- Generating a response (LLM)
- Converting text back to speech (TTS)
- Sending audio back to the user

## Why humans notice it so easily

Human conversation has a natural rhythm — that ~200 millisecond gap between turns mentioned earlier. When AI takes noticeably longer than that, it doesn't just feel slow. It breaks the illusion that you're having a conversation at all.

<Callout>
Below ~300ms, a pause feels natural. Above ~1 second, it starts to feel like the system isn't listening.
</Callout>

## How systems compensate

Since removing all delay isn't possible, well-designed voice AI hides it instead:

- Streaming responses word-by-word instead of waiting for the full answer
- Starting to speak before the entire reply is generated
- Using short acknowledgment sounds or phrases while thinking

None of these make the AI faster — they make the wait feel shorter, which is often just as important.
