Many speakers move between languages mid-sentence without noticing — a habit often called code-switching. For voice AI, this is one of the hardest problems to solve well.

## What has to happen

Handling multiple languages isn't just translation. The system has to:

- Detect which language is being spoken, in real time
- Recognize when a speaker switches languages mid-sentence
- Choose a voice and accent that matches the response language
- Keep meaning and tone consistent across the switch

## Why this is harder than it sounds

Speech-to-Text and Text-to-Speech models are often trained per-language. Switching languages mid-conversation can mean switching models entirely — which reintroduces the latency problem from the previous lesson, at exactly the moment it's least forgivable.

<Callout>
A system that handles one language flawlessly can still feel broken the moment a user mixes in a second one.
</Callout>

## Why it matters for design, not just engineering

In many multilingual regions, code-switching isn't an edge case — it's the default way people actually talk. Designing voice AI only for "clean," single-language input means designing for a version of conversation that doesn't reflect how a large share of users actually speak.
